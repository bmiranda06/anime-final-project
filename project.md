# Anime Final Project Plan

## Core Thesis

**Question:** What do Otomo Katsuhiro's *Akira*, Anno Hideaki's *Neon Genesis Evangelion*, Kon Satoshi's *Paprika*, and Oshii Mamoru's *Ghost in the Shell 2: Innocence* reveal about Japanese culture?

**Thesis:** These anime reveal that modern Japanese culture often imagines identity as unstable and relational, shaped by the body, technology, dreams, and collective systems rather than by one fixed individual self.

**One-line argument:** The webapp turns identity into a playable problem: each layer gives only a partial version of the self, so the player must move between body, machine, dream, and network to realize that no single layer fully explains who they are.

## What Will Earn Points

- The project must present a **novel, insightful interpretation** of one theme across animated works.
- It should clearly connect the creative work to **historical/social context, plot, and artistic media**.
- It needs to expand class discussion by making connections among course anime and outside research.
- The final summary will need a precise title, 3-5 keywords, the required question/answer structure, in-text citations, and a bibliography.
- The strongest move: design the game around **three creative aspects**, because the rubric asks us to explain three aspects of the project that support the thesis.

## Three Creative Aspects

1. **Layer Switching:** The same world appears differently in the Body, Artificial Body, Dream, and Network layers. This makes fragmented identity a mechanic, not just a claim.
2. **Contradictory Evidence:** Each layer gives the player memories, logs, symbols, or social traces that conflict with the others, forcing interpretation.
3. **Player-Chosen Ending:** The ending depends on which evidence the player trusts: identity as bodily, fragmented, or collective.

## Game Layout

- **Opening:** The player wakes up with no stable identity and only one available layer.
- **Hub World:** One shared map/space that changes visually and narratively depending on the selected layer.
- **Body Layer - *Akira*:** Physical limits, power, emotion, social bonds, and bodily instability.
- **Artificial Body Layer - *Evangelion*:** Machine interface, psychological fracture, synchronization, and blurred self/machine boundaries.
- **Dream Layer - *Paprika*:** Memory, desire, dream logic, symbolic identity, and emotional truth.
- **Network Layer - *Ghost in the Shell 2: Innocence*:** Distributed consciousness, data traces, dolls/bodies, and collective identity.
- **Pathways:** Objects unlock movement between layers: bed to dream, terminal/cable to network, cockpit/interface to artificial body, mirror/body scan to physical self.
- **Final Choice:** The player assembles evidence and chooses the interpretation of identity they believe is strongest.

## High-Level Build Plan

- Build a polished HTML/CSS/JavaScript webapp with a strong visual identity for each layer.
- Keep gameplay simple but meaningful: exploration, item collection, layer switching, evidence comparison, and three endings.
- Make every major mechanic easy to explain later in the required 1000-1500 word final summary.
- Track sources for the essay from the start, especially course readings on *Akira*, *Evangelion*, *Paprika*, and *Ghost in the Shell 2: Innocence*.
- Cite any AI help visibly in the final written component, per the syllabus.
